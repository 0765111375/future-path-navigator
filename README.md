# Future Path Navigator

Yes — this is a much stronger hackathon direction.

Your mentors have essentially helped you turn NextPath from a broad career platform into a specific, measurable solution:

NextPath helps Grade 9 learners, especially those in rural and underserved communities, make informed subject and career decisions by giving them a personalised roadmap from Grade 9 to a future career.

That makes the product easier to build, easier to explain, and much easier for judges to remember.

The key is that NextPath should not tell a learner "you should become X." It should say:

"Based on what you've told us, here are your strongest pathways, why they fit, what you need to improve, and what alternatives are available."

1. 🔥 THE NEW NEXTPath CONCEPT

Problem

A Grade 9 learner is being asked to make one of the most important academic decisions of their life — choosing subjects — without necessarily knowing what careers exist, what those careers require, or where their current abilities could take them.

This is especially difficult for learners in rural and underserved communities, where exposure to diverse career paths and access to career guidance can be limited.

A learner might think:

"I want to work with computers."

But they don't know:

What careers exist in technology?

What subjects should I choose?

What marks do I need?

Which university courses lead there?

What skills should I start developing?

What projects can I do?

What happens if I don't qualify?

What other careers are similar?

That's where NextPath comes in.
